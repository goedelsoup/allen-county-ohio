**The installation was begun in May 1942, nine years before the deed this corpus had read.** The
Ohio Steel Foundry Company began building a government-owned, contractor-operated plant about five
miles south of the centre of Lima, on open land previously used for agriculture, to produce
centrifugally cast gun tubes. [verified] — the historic properties report, *World War II to 1950*.
The Army cancelled that mission within the year, before the plant was finished, because a new
process for piercing seamless tubing made the casting plant unnecessary, and diverted the site to a
depot for modifying and processing tanks and other combat vehicles.

**It grew, shrank and grew again, and the acreages are the thread.** 170 acres at the start; 163
after a 7-acre sale in 1943, and unchanged until 1951; 458 by June 1951 after the Army bought 295
acres of contiguous parcels *just south-east of the original base*; 373 by the mid-1970s after land
was released to an Army Reserve training centre and to Johnny Appleseed Park. [verified] — the same
report. The 1951 purchase is an expansion of a nine-year-old installation and not its founding, and
the private dwellings that came with that land are still numbered buildings on it.

**The contractor-operators are named in sequence.** Ohio Steel Foundry from May 1942; United Motors
Service, a General Motors subsidiary, from November 1942; the Chrysler Corporation, later Chrysler
Defense, from the M-1 award; and General Dynamics from March 1982, when Chrysler sold Chrysler
Defense to it for $348.5 million. [verified] — the same report. That is the corporate chain this
corpus had one end of.

**The names of the installation are a chronology in themselves.** Lima Tank Depot in the war; Lima
Ordnance Depot from late 1945; Lima Army Modification Center through the 1970s; Lima Army Tank
Plant on the M-1 award. [verified] — the same report.

**The archeological report is a report about nothing having been looked for.** It "documents the
lack of any known archeological sites on the facility" and states that no archeological
investigation has ever been conducted on installation land, recommending a reconnaissance survey of
a 53-acre parcel then scheduled for construction. [verified] — the archeological overview, abstract
and recommendations. An absence of sites in a file that records no survey is an absence of looking;
see [a zero is not a blank](../decisions/a-zero-is-not-a-blank.yml).

**Neither report is disinterested and both say so.** They exist to bring the installation into
compliance with the National Historic Preservation Act, and the buildings report's finding — that
there are no Category I, II or III historic properties at Lima — is the finding that imposes no
obligation on the Army. [verified] — the historic properties report, *Preservation
Recommendations*. The chronology it gathers on the way to that conclusion is not thereby suspect,
and the conclusion is read here as a determination rather than as a fact about the buildings.

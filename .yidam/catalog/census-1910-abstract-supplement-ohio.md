**It completes the county's decennial series.** Allen County was **56,580** in 1910, against 47,976
in 1900 and 40,644 in 1890 — the last two of which this corpus already held from
[the 1906 county history](miller-allen-county-1906.md), so the two known columns fix the reading of
the third. With this figure the county has a population for every census from 1830 to 2020 and an
estimate for 2024, with no gaps. [verified]

**Three columns, three closures.** Allen County's thirteen minor civil divisions sum to the printed
county total in every one of the three census years — 56,580, 47,976 and 40,644 — with no residual.
Lima's four wards sum to its 30,508. A table that closes three ways is not a table anyone
transcribed loosely.

**It measures Ottawa Township, by name, in a federal source.** The row reads "Ottawa township,
coextensive with Lima city", and gives 30,508, 21,723 and 15,987 against the city's 30,508, 21,723
and **15,981**. The six-person difference at 1890 is not a misprint: without it the 1890 column does
not sum to the county total. So the two were exactly equal at 1900 and 1910 and six people apart in
1890, and the coextension this corpus has described since genesis is datable to between those
censuses. See
[what became of German and Ottawa townships](../corpus/question/what-became-of-german-and-ottawa-townships.yml).

**Its Allen County footnote records four boundary events in two directions**, and is the earliest
annexation evidence the corpus holds:

> ¹ Parts of German and Bath townships annexed to Lima city in 1902 and 1905, respectively; parts
> of Lima city annexed to Bath and German townships in 1903 and 1906, respectively.

The embedded text layer reads the second pair as 1003 and 1006, which is impossible; a separate
optical run reads 1903 and 1906. Two digitizations and a semantic check, which is the method
[two scans of one book](../decisions/two-scans-of-one-book.yml) sets out.

**Its manufactures chapter gave this corpus its first measurement of work.** Two tables report Lima
independently and agree: the comparative city table on printed page 700 gives average wage earners
and value of products for 1909, 1904 and 1899, and the detail table for *cities of 10,000 to 50,000
inhabitants* on printed page 754 breaks 1909 into establishments, proprietors, clerks, wage earners
by sex and age, and primary horsepower. The detail row's components sum to its own totals twice
over, and its 3,607 wage earners match the comparative table. See
[Lima manufactures](../corpus/measure/lima-manufactures-1899-1909.yml).

The prose around those tables carries three things the tables do not: Lima's leading industries in
1909 were "tobacco manufactures and locomotives"; the city was in the group whose products were
valued between $5,000,000 and $10,000,000; and it was one of five Ohio cities out of thirty-seven
whose product value **fell** between 1899 and 1904.

**What else is in it, unread, with page numbers this time.** The agriculture chapter runs county
columns across the page rather than county rows down it, and holds at least: *Table 2, number,
acreage and value of farms classified by tenure*; *Table 3, live stock products and domestic
animals*; *Table 4, value of all crops and principal classes thereof*; and *Table 6, number and
value of domestic animals not on farms, by counties*. Allen County is the second column in each,
after Adams. There is also a mines and quarries chapter and, in the population section, tables on
school attendance, illiteracy, marital condition and nativity. No occupations table: the abstract
says so itself. The corpus has now read two chapters of five. See
[a source is not exhausted by one question](../decisions/a-source-is-not-exhausted-by-one-question.yml).

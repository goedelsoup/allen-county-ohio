**What it is.** A special report of the Bureau of the Census covering the electric railway industry
for the calendar year 1917, with comparative tables for 1912, 1907 and 1902. The corpus holds it for
one thing: the directory at the back, described in the volume's own words as listing the operating
companies "alphabetically, by states, with the lessor companies in italics immediately following the
company that leases and operates the road".

**Its classification is two letters and both of them are evidence.** The first letter is size, by
income from railway operations: **A** is over \$1,000,000, **B** is over \$250,000 to \$1,000,000,
**C** is \$250,000 and less. The second is character of business: **X** is a railway with no light
and power department, **Y** a railway that "generate[s] and sell[s] electric energy for light,
power, and other purposes", **Z** miscellaneous. An asterisk marks the 110 companies carried in the
comparative summary for 1917 and 1912.

**What it says about Allen County, in three lines of an index.** The Ohio pages carry
`*Ohio Electric Ry. Co., The — A-Y`, with five lessor companies in italics beneath it, one of them
`Fort Wayne, Van Wert & Lima Traction Co., The`; and, further down the same list,
`Western Ohio Ry. Co. — B-X`, standing on its own. That is the corporate fate of both of the
interurbans [the 1921 history](rusler-allen-county-1921.md) says served Lima, told by a source that
had no interest in Lima and no reason to flatter it.

**Why it is worth a catalog entry for three lines.** The two county histories are the corpus's only
narrative of the interurban, and neither can say what happened to a company after its own printing
date. This volume can, because it is a census rather than a chronicle: it was compiled once, from
returns, for every company in the country, and a company's absence from it is nearly as informative
as its presence. The Lima & Toledo Traction Company, which
[the 1906 history](miller-allen-county-1906.md) presents as the coming thing, is not in it under
that name.

**What it does not carry.** No mileage, revenue or employment for any individual company — the
directory gives names and classes only, and the tables that carry figures are aggregated by state
and by class. So it can confirm that the Western Ohio bought its current and the Ohio Electric sold
it, and cannot check the 1921 history's claim that the Ohio Electric operated 600 miles of railway.
Nothing in it is county-level; Allen County appears in it only inside a company's name.

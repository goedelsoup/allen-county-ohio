  The two federal accounts written about the outbreak of 11 April 1965 rather than filed about it:
  the Weather Bureau's survey-team report of May 1965, which flew the damage and printed the office

  identified every track from the air and grouped them into families. Between them they settle
  where this county's tornado stopped, what it belonged to, and why nobody saw it.

**Fujita named this county's tornado L-4 and gave it a family.** It is the fourth of six tornadoes
thrown off one parent echo — "the first of six that covered a total distance of 274 mi in 4 hr 23
min, maintaining an amazingly constant speed of 62.5 mi hr⁻¹" — running from south-east of Lafayette,
Indiana to Strongsville, Ohio. Of L-4 itself: "The damage by tornado L-4 was first spotted east of
US-30S, northwest of Lima, Ohio. The storm then continued beyond Cairo, Ohio. The damage was
moderate to light." And then: "With a path width varying between ¼ and ½ mi, the tornado left
typical explosive damage to structures and wind-blown debris. **No funnel was sighted by local
residents because of darkness. The last damage was spotted northeast of Houcktown, Ohio.**" —
Fujita et al. 1970, page 42.

**Which settles an endpoint two federal files disagreed about.** *Storm Data* stopped the track "one
mile north of Jenera" at 25 miles; the Storm Events and SPC files carry it 32.5 miles to a point
1.1 miles from Houcktown. An aircraft flying the damage five days later put the last damage
north-east of Houcktown. The databases are right about where it stopped and the book is short by
seven miles.

**And it dissolves the publication's reason for separating two tornadoes.** *Storm Data* wrote that
this tornado's path "lines up with the one that passed through Willshire, but could not have been
the same one because both occured at the same time" (the misspelling is the publication's). Fujita's
L-3 is the Willshire tornado and L-4 is this one: not two simultaneous storms but consecutive members
of one family, from one parent, about fourteen minutes apart at 62.5 miles an hour.

**The survey team's report is the earliest federal record of a death in this county, and it says
three or four.** Its Annex 2 reprints the Columbus office's statement issued at 1:00 a.m. EST, some
three and a half hours after the tornado: "Highway Patrol reports tornadoes hit hard in the Toledo
area...19 confirmed fatalities in this area with more expected...**3 or 4 fatalities have been
reported in Cairo which lies just north of Lima Ohio.**"

**Its own catalogue of thirty-seven tornadoes leaves this one out.** Table 2 numbers thirty-seven
entries by time and place, and the two nearest are "near Van Wert (28 ESE Fort Wayne), Ind." and
"4 S Tiffin (23 E Findlay) - Ohio". There is no Lima, Cairo, Bluffton, Jenera or Allen County row —
in a report whose figure 1 gives Ohio 59 deaths and $42,500,000 of damage, and the five states 271
between them. **The county's deadliest event is missing from the federal survey of the outbreak that
caused it.**

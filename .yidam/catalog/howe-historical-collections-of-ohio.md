**The OCR of both scans separates words with two spaces and every search here was run against a
whitespace-normalised copy.** A grep for "county seat" returns nothing on either file and
"county  seat" returns the whole book. Any negative result taken from these texts without that
normalisation is a fact about the search. [verified] — both routes exercised, 1 September 2026.

**What makes this volume worth cataloging is the formation sentence, which is a fixed form.**
Every county entry opens with one, and it carries the same fields in the same order: when the
county was formed, what it was formed from, and — in about half the entries — who or what it was
named from. Because the form is fixed, an entry that omits the namesake is a statement about what
Howe knew, and can be read against the entries that do not omit it. [inference]

**Howe is not a primary source and the corpus does not treat him as one.** He was compiling in
1846 from travel, correspondence and whatever local record he could reach, twenty-six years after
the act this corpus wants. What he gives is the earliest surviving printed attribution, which is a
different thing from the legislature's reason. [inference]

**Its preamble names the court's divisions, and there are five of them:**

> In accordance with Article IV, Section V(B) of the Ohio Constitution, Civil Rule 83, and Criminal
> Rule 57, the following Rules of Court for practice and procedure in the General Trial Division,
> Criminal Division, Domestic Relations Division, and where applicable, the Juvenile Division and
> Probate Division of this Court, are hereby adopted, effective February 1, 2023.

[verified] That is a different count from the three the county's own site gives — General, Domestic
Relations, and Probate and Juvenile — and a different count again from the four the elections roster
implies by assigning judges to General, Domestic and Probate.

The three are not in conflict so much as answering different questions: the rules divide *practice*,
the county's site divides *offices with their own web presence*, and the roster divides *seats on a
ballot*. The corpus records the rules' five because they are the court's own, and records that the
Criminal Division has no separately elected judge. [inference]

**It does not say how many judges sit.** The rules speak of "the judges of the Court" and an
"administrative judge", and give a rule for what happens when "companion cases have been assigned to
different judges", so more than one is certain and the number is not stated. [verified] The count
comes from [the elections roster](ohio-boe-elected-officials-allen.md) instead.

**What else is in it, unread.** Sixty-odd rules across roughly 6,400 lines of extracted text: case
management, jury management, costs and fees, discovery, foreclosure, criminal arraignment,
mediation. It is a full account of how a case moves through this county's trial court, and this
corpus has taken two paragraphs from it.

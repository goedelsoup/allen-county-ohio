**What it is.** The middle volume of the county's three histories, and the only one written by local
men: Charles C. Miller, Ph.D., and Dr. Samuel A. Baxter, both of Lima, for a Chicago subscription
publisher. Same genre as
[the 1885 book](leeson-allen-county-1885.md) and
[the 1921 book](rusler-allen-county-1921.md) — narrative plus paid biography — and the same rule
applies: the biography is bought and this corpus takes nothing from it.

**It is here for one chapter and it earns the retrieval in that chapter alone.** Its geology section
carries `THE FIRST OIL WELL IN ALLEN COUNTY` and `THE SECOND OIL-WELL`, and they are not
reminiscence. They give the completion date, the site, the contractors by name, the casing
elevation, the depth to the Trenton limestone, what the drill found in the shale and what it did not
find in the limestone, the shooting, the first six days' yield in barrels, the contractor's own
estimate of the well's rate, and the colour, gravity and smell of the oil. Written twenty-one years
after the event by two Lima men, one of them a physician who is also in
[the 1885 book's](leeson-allen-county-1885.md) index.

It also does something neither of the others does: it says **the famous well failed**. The pioneer
well "meeting with a series of misfortunes that left it useless" is a sentence the 1921 volume —
which builds a page of rhetoric on the same well — does not contain.

**Two scans, so the two-scan rule runs here.** Both are Internet Archive digitizations of different
library copies, and every figure this corpus takes from the oil chapter was read in both and agrees:
May 9 1885, 1,250 feet, 200 barrels in six days, the 18-barrel estimate, 1,450 barrels in December
1885. Scan A's OCR is broken by long runs of garbage from facing plates; scan B is cleaner and more
complete. See [two-scans-of-one-book](../decisions/two-scans-of-one-book.yml).

**What it does not carry — checked, not assumed.** Nothing after 1906, so no answer to the 1918
renaming of German Township or Ottawa Township's end. No erection date for Bath Township. No
namesake for the county. Its account of the refinery's siting is thinner than the 1921 book's, which
is the one place the later volume is the better source.

**What it carries that this phase did not take.** A chapter on the Welsh settlement of Gomer, a
population series for Ohio and Allen County running back before 1885, city officials of Lima, and a
township-by-township account that would cross-check the 1885 book at every point.

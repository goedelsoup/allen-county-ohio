**What it is.** The middle volume of the county's three histories, and the only one written by local
men: Charles C. Miller, Ph.D., and Dr. Samuel A. Baxter, both of Lima, for a Chicago subscription
publisher. Same genre as
[the 1885 book](leeson-allen-county-1885.md) and
[the 1921 book](rusler-allen-county-1921.md) — narrative plus paid biography — and the same rule
applies: the biography is bought and this corpus takes nothing from it.

**It is here for one chapter and it earns the retrieval in that chapter alone.** Its geology section
carries `THE FIRST OIL WELL IN ALLEN COUNTY` and `THE SECOND OIL-WELL`, and they are not
reminiscence. They give the completion date, the site, the contractors by name, the casing
elevation, the depth to the Trenton limestone, what the drill found in the shale and what it did not
find in the limestone, the shooting, the first six days' yield in barrels, the contractor's own
estimate of the well's rate, and the colour, gravity and smell of the oil. Written twenty-one years
after the event by two Lima men, one of them a physician who is also in
[the 1885 book's](leeson-allen-county-1885.md) index.

It also does something neither of the others does: it says **the famous well failed**. The pioneer
well "meeting with a series of misfortunes that left it useless" is a sentence the 1921 volume —
which builds a page of rhetoric on the same well — does not contain.

**Two scans, so the two-scan rule runs here.** Both are Internet Archive digitizations of different
library copies, and every figure this corpus takes from the oil chapter was read in both and agrees:
May 9 1885, 1,250 feet, 200 barrels in six days, the 18-barrel estimate, 1,450 barrels in December
1885. Scan A's OCR is broken by long runs of garbage from facing plates; scan B is cleaner and more
complete. See [two-scans-of-one-book](../decisions/two-scans-of-one-book.yml).

**What it does not carry — checked, not assumed.** Nothing after 1906, so no answer to the 1918
renaming of German Township or Ottawa Township's end. No erection date for Bath Township. No
namesake for the county. Its account of the refinery's siting is thinner than the 1921 book's, which
is the one place the later volume is the better source.

**What it carries that this phase did not take.** A chapter on the Welsh settlement of Gomer, a
population series for Ohio and Allen County running back before 1885, city officials of Lima, and a
township-by-township account that would cross-check the 1885 book at every point.

**Its Civil War statistics are quoted, and it names a source the catalog does not hold.** The whole
statistical passage — Camp Lima, the Eighth Military District, the enrollment of 3,792, the 1,411
enlisted, the 163 Squirrel Hunters, the roster total of 1,920 — is set in quotation marks and
introduced as "From the 'Allen County History' of 1875 we take the following". The text inside the
quotation marks is [the 1885 history](leeson-allen-county-1885.md) word for word, including its
total of its own roster, which a book of 1875 could not have printed.

Either the attribution is wrong or both books drew on something earlier that this catalog does not
hold — the 1921 history mentions Horace S. Knapp's Allen County data in a world atlas of 1875 and a
Historical Sketch of Allen County of 1880, and holds neither. The consequence for the corpus is the
same in both cases: **on the Civil War this volume is not an independent witness**, and any figure
it shares with the 1885 book is one witness, not two. See
[what the county sent](../corpus/measure/allen-county-civil-war-service.yml).

**Where it does speak for itself about the war, it introduces errors.** Outside the quotation it
writes "Of the 100,224 common soldiers which Ohio sent to the war, Allen County furnished 776",
dropping the "in 1861" that the 1885 book puts in the same sentence and turning a first-year cohort
into a whole-war total — an error the 1921 history then inherits and sets against a different
figure as though the two conflicted. It also converts a statement about the *First Ohio Volunteer
Infantry* into one about this county ("in just 60 hours thereafter soldiers from this county were
en route for Washington"), and dates Isaiah S. Pillars' appointment as commander of Camp Lima to
1861, a year in which — by the dates in the passage it has just quoted — there was no Camp Lima to
command.

**What else is in it, still unread.** Its biographical volume is full of war service — Squirrel
Hunters, the Sultana, deaths at Corinth and Shiloh — attached to named men, at a level of detail
the 1885 roster's four fields cannot carry.

**One of its two authors was probably mayor of Lima in 1851.** The title page gives Charles C.
Miller and Dr. Samuel A. Baxter, "both of Lima"; the 1885 history gives a Samuel A. Baxter elected
mayor in 1851, appointed mayor in January 1854 in place of a man who had resigned, elected again
that year and re-elected in 1855, and serving as surgeon of the 18th Ohio from March 1863 to
November 1865. The 1921 history says Baxter "contributed a number of reminiscent chapters" to this
volume.

A physician who was mayor at twenty-six would have been past eighty when this book appeared, which
is old and not impossible, and reminiscent chapters are what a man of that age contributes. The
corpus records it as probable and not shown — no source held here gives either man a date of birth
— and notes the consequence either way: **the geology chapter this corpus has leaned on hardest,
the technical account of the first oil well, was written by men who were in the town when it was
drilled**, and on this reading one of them had been its mayor thirty-four years earlier. See
[Samuel A. Baxter](../corpus/person/samuel-a-baxter.yml) and
[the line of mayors](../corpus/measure/lima-mayors-1842-1886.yml).

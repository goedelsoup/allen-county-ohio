**It supplies the vintage the roster leaves out**, and the vintage turns out to be the whole
question. Ohio's state legislative map was adopted by the Ohio Redistricting Commission on
**29 September 2023**, unanimously, and "will govern for the rest of the decade"; challenges to it
were rejected. Its congressional map was adopted **31 October 2025**, unanimously, by the state's
backup commission, and is "valid for the rest of the decade". [verified]

**Those two dates fall either side of the November 2024 election, and that is why this source
matters here.** Ohio's sitting members of Congress were elected in November 2024 under the map that
the October 2025 plan replaced. So a roster naming Allen County's present representative names
someone chosen under a superseded map, and does not establish which district the county is in now.
Its state senator and state representative, elected the same day, *were* chosen under the map still
in force. Same roster, same election, two different vintages. See
[which congressional district Allen County is in now](../corpus/question/allen-county-current-congressional-district.yml).

**What it does not carry.** No boundaries and no county lists — it dates maps and does not draw
them. It cannot say which counties are in a district, which is the fact that
[the block assignment files](census-block-assignment-2020.md) supplied for 2020 and that no
retrievable source has supplied since.
